    It's official, it's the UK's annual May heatwave! #blametheweather In eCommerce, we’ve all had the same conversations. I hope we all know that: a) Hot (by UK standards!) weather definitely can affect sales. b) Weather can definitely be used as an excuse for lazy analysis - or simply masking that we don't know what happened yesterday. Set aside the obvious categories: BBQs, sun cream, AC units, swimsuits, garden supplies. The real challenge is when you’re selling something broadly weather-neutral - like broadband, fridges, or jewellery. We run campaigns for many hundreds of ecommerce and retail businesses, so we see a lot of data. And even then, it’s not always easy to tell what’s going on. What we see: ☀️ Hot weather changes behaviour more than demand. During the 40°C heatwave in July 2022, online traffic stayed broadly flat - but sales fell by 30.7% over two days. People were browsing… just not buying. ☀️ Then the rebound kicks in. As soon as the heat broke, sales jumped +51% the very next day (vs the previous Wednesday). So those purchases weren’t lost - just postponed (in theory). ☀️ And over time, things normalise. According to IMRG, in weather-neutral sectors, behaviour stabilises within a few days. In other words: we get used to the heat, and buying patterns return to baseline. So how do you know if it was the weather? 💥 Dig into your data. Is it demand, CTR, conversion rate, or site performance? Find the metric that's changed. 💥 Don’t start with the weather - end with it. It’s a conclusion, not an excuse. Rule out broken tracking, misfiring ads, slow load times, promo fatigue - and all the usual self-inflicted wounds. 💥 Use regional variance to your advantage. Hot in Cambridge, cool in Cardiff? That’s a natural control group. Segment by location if you can. 💥 Benchmark if you’re able. Access to same-day, cross-retailer performance is rare - but invaluable. Genie retailers & ecomm clients can ask us. Otherwise, good old-fashioned peer conversations still go a long way. If you can confidently attribute performance to the weather - and understand which metrics it affects - that gives you an edge. You might even use it proactively next time (e.g. in bidding or budgeting decisions). At the very least understanding performance helps your quality of sleep. If you’re anything like me, you’ve probably got a few rain dances and ritual sacrifices up your sleeve to bring back the British weather and get those fridge sales back on track! Very interested to hears others take on the inevitable weather vs performance question. #ecommerce #onlineretail #datadriven #conversionrate #weatherimpact #retailstrategy

    FMCG companies which don’t shift from 'Transaction' to 'Customer Lifetime Value' mindset will soon find themselves at a massive disadvantage... I’ve seen this very clearly in my recent work at Sodastream — where adopting SaaS-style thinking fundamentally changes how we build value. For years, FMCG has focused on penetration, frequency, volume per purchase and price per item. These are important, but they only describe singular transactions. They don’t explain the economics of growth nor the long-term value creation in the world of hyper-connectivity. Meanwhile, SaaS companies (Software as a Service) use a completely different mental model — and it’s one FMCG urgently needs to learn from. Over the past months, I’ve found myself speaking a new language: CAC, CLTV, Churn Rate, Retention, CAC Payback, Referral Loops. It may sound like tech jargon, but these concepts are incredibly powerful for FMCG. Here’s why: - Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) forces you to look at the real cost of bringing a new customer into your ecosystem — not just the media spend, but all investments required to convert them. - Churn highlights the percentage of consumers who “leave” — who stop buying, stop engaging, or switch away. FMCG rarely measures this properly. - Customer Lifetime Value (CLTV) estimates the total value a consumer will generate over their lifetime with your brand. This moves thinking away from single purchases toward recurring behaviour and long-term relationships. At Sodastream, this mindset became very real.  The investment leading to Sparkling Water Maker purchase is essentially our CAC — the cost of bringing someone into the system.  The recurring CO2 Cylinders and Flavours represent the CLTV — the long-term value and the core of our business model. And here is the real accelerant of this transformation for all FMCG: With the rapid rise of e-Commerce and DTC, we are finally gaining access to deep, vertical First-Party Data — real purchasing patterns, replenishment cycles, early churn signals, bundle potential, upgrade behaviour, advocacy triggers. This is the type of data you need to actually measure lifetime value, not just guess it. Layer onto that the new generation of AI Models capable of large-scale personalisation — predicting next purchase, identifying at-risk consumers, tailoring offers, generating relevant content and nudging behaviour at exactly the right moment — and suddenly the old FMCG transaction model starts to look obsolete… The future of FMCG belongs to the brands that think more like SaaS.  FMCG brands that embrace this shift will create more resilient, profitable and predictable growth engines. Those that don’t - will keep fighting for every single transaction - fading out from exhaustion. #CLTV #CAC #FMCG #MarketingTransformation #CMO #DigitalGrowth #Retention #Ecommerce #DTC #SaaS #FutureOfMarketing
