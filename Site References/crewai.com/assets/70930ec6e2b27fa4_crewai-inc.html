    Had a great conversation with John Furrier on theCUBE recently about something I keep seeing with our enterprise customers. Everyone talks about the "last mile" of getting AI agents into production. But when you actually sit with Fortune 500 companies and understand what it takes to go from a model being ready to actual business impact — the last mile is more like a hundred miles. Here's what I mean. The models are incredible. They keep getting better. And yes, as they improve, teams are removing scaffolding and allocating more to the model itself. But between "the model works" and "this is impacting our bottom line," there's a massive gap: - Security and governance requirements - Data preparation and labeling - Integration complexity (some of our customers have 30+ instances of SAP alone) - PII handling and role-based access control - Ensuring data that was previously untapped is actually ready to be surfaced This is why building agents is quickly becoming a commodity — there are so many ways to build them now. But there is zero ROI, zero impact on the bottom line, if you just build. The value comes from running these things in production at scale. And that's where the real requirements start showing up. What we're seeing at CrewAI is that enterprises need four things: 1. Central visibility and control 2. Reusable building blocks so you're not rebuilding the same agents twice across global teams 3. Flexibility to leverage existing infrastructure, models, and systems 4. Agent-to-agent communication over the wire — A2A is accelerating faster than most people expected The companies getting the most ROI right now aren't the ones with the most agents. They're the ones being most savvy about the operational layer. Our numbers reflect this — average selling price has 11x'd, and we're seeing customers expand 12x within a year. For every dollar we close, there's another 11-12 behind it. The market is moving too fast to pick winners. That's why companies are favoring open source and optionality. They want to make progress while keeping the door open. If you're a CIO or enterprise leader right now, that's probably the most important strategic bet you can make.
